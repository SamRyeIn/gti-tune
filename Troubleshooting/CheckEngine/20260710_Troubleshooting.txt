2017 VW GTI DSG - 2026-07-10 Check-Engine / DSG Scan Troubleshooting
=====================================================================

Source scan: 20260710_Codes.txt

IMPORTANT
---------
Do not perform further WOT pulls or ECU/TCU calibration validation until the
DSG fault state is characterized. If PRNDS flashes, drive/reverse cannot be
selected reliably, or the DSG enters limp mode, do not drive the car except as
needed to park it safely or load it for transport.

Recorded DTCs
-------------
ECU
  P0036 - Bank 1 Sensor 2 oxygen-sensor heater control-circuit fault.

DSG
  P072C - Stuck in gear 1.
  P072B - Stuck in reverse.
  P174A - Valve 3 in transmission section 1 electrical fault.
  P174E - Valve 3 in transmission section 2 electrical fault.
  P0726 - Engine-speed input circuit range/performance.
  P179D - Transmission-oil cooling-valve electrical fault.
  P1734 - Selector-lever sensor-system starter-release fault.

Assessment
----------
The DSG faults are the priority. P072C and P072B are consequences of the
faulted DSG system, not the first repair target. P174A, P174E, and P179D are
explicit electrical faults across multiple DSG actuator circuits. This pattern
most strongly indicates a shared electrical cause:

  1. Low-voltage event, weak battery, charging-system fault, or poor power/
     ground connection.
  2. DSG mechatronic external connector or harness damage, corrosion, fluid
     ingress, or loose connection.
  3. Internal mechatronic circuit-board, solenoid, or control-module failure.

The Cobb DSG tune is not the leading explanation. A TCU calibration can affect
shift behavior, torque limits, and clutch-pressure behavior, but should not
create electrical-circuit faults in multiple valve circuits. Do not reflash the
TCU as the first diagnostic action. Do not flash either ECU or TCU while system
voltage is questionable.

The ECU and DSG faults are treated as independent until evidence shows
otherwise. None of the documented SimosTools ECU calibration changes plausibly
causes a DSG valve electrical fault.

P0036 Assessment
----------------
P0036 is the heater circuit for the rear/post-catalyst oxygen sensor, Bank 1
Sensor 2. A performance-oriented catalytic converter can cause a catalyst
efficiency DTC (commonly P0420), but does not normally cause a heater-circuit
DTC by itself.

The car previously had an O2 sensor unplugged while Cobb tuned. If that was the
rear/post-catalyst sensor, reconnecting it is the most likely direct resolution
for P0036. The sensor, connector, any extension harness, and wiring near the
downpipe must still be inspected for exhaust heat damage, stretching, pinching,
or corrosion. Do not suppress P0036 in the ECU calibration.

Recommended Diagnostic Sequence
-------------------------------
1. Preserve evidence before clearing anything.
   - Obtain a full VCDS or OBDeleven Auto-Scan.
   - Save DSG controller identification, all DTC status bits, fault frequency,
     mileage/time stamps, and freeze-frame or environmental data.
   - The source scan identifies the codes but not whether faults are static,
     intermittent, or associated with low voltage.

2. Check electrical-system health.
   - Test the battery and record resting voltage after the vehicle has sat.
   - Record charging voltage with the engine running.
   - Inspect battery terminals, main fuses, battery ground, and engine/
     transmission ground straps.

3. Inspect the DSG electrical path with the vehicle safely off and using the
   factory procedure.
   - Inspect the external mechatronic/DSG connector and harness.
   - Look for a loose connector lock, damaged pins, corrosion, fluid ingress,
     chafing, or rodent damage.
   - Do not jumper or power DSG solenoids without the vehicle-specific wiring
     diagram and the appropriate guided-test procedure.

4. Address the rear O2-sensor connection.
   - With the exhaust cold, identify and fully reconnect Bank 1 Sensor 2.
   - Inspect the connector and downstream-sensor wiring/extension.
   - Preserve the scan, clear the ECU DTC, key-cycle, and rescan.
   - Complete a normal cold start and short non-WOT drive, then scan again.
   - P0036 returning immediately or after the cold start points to the sensor
     heater, connector/extension, wiring, fuse/feed, or ground.
   - P0036 remaining absent after reconnection requires no ECU calibration
     change. A later P0420 would be a separate catalyst-efficiency issue.

5. After preserving the full scan and completing basic voltage/connector checks,
   clear the DSG faults and key-cycle without driving.
   - DSG electrical faults returning immediately as static favor the connector,
     harness, or internal mechatronic electrical path.
   - Faults returning only with a voltage, heat, vibration, or drive-cycle event
     require controlled diagnosis with live DSG supply voltage and fault status.
   - Do not use WOT testing to reproduce DSG faults.

Web Research Notes
------------------
Web results corroborate that P174A and P174E identify electrical faults in
Valve 3 for the two DSG transmission sections. A DQ250 case containing the
same core electrical combination (P0726, P174A, P174E, and P179D) was located.
This supports investigation of the mechatronic electrical path, but is not
evidence to replace a mechatronic unit before testing.

Current Decision
----------------
Pause tuning and high-load operation. First reconnect and verify the rear O2
sensor path for P0036, then obtain the detailed DSG scan and diagnose the
shared electrical path before considering any DSG hardware replacement or TCU
reflash.
